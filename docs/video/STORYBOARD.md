# YouTube video plan: Spooky Treat Station

**Working title:** *I Built an AI Halloween Screen That Scares Trick-or-Treaters (Kids Get BOO, Adults Get TERRIFIED)*

**Format:** one main video (about 7 minutes) plus 2–3 Shorts cut from the same footage.
**Goal:** a strong first hour. On Halloween week this mostly depends on the **thumbnail, title, the first 15 seconds, and posting a Short first**. No video is guaranteed 1,000 views in an hour, but the plan below gives it the best chance.

---

## 1. Title options (A/B test the top two with YouTube's "Test & Compare")

1. I Built an AI Halloween Screen That Scares Trick-or-Treaters 😱
2. My Front Door Knows If You're a Kid or an Adult… (Halloween AI Prank)
3. This $0 AI Halloween Display Scared Everyone on My Street
4. Kids Get "BOO", Adults Get NIGHTMARES – DIY AI Halloween Screen
5. I Made My Candy Bowl Fight Back (Free, Runs on an Old PC)

## 2. Thumbnail (most important asset)

* **Left half:** a real reaction shot of an adult jumping back, mouth open (or you acting it out).
* **Right half:** the screaming face from the screen, big and glitchy red.
* **Small inset:** the friendly cartoon ghost with a kid-safe "BOO!", which shows the kid/adult twist.
* **Text, three words max:** **"KIDS vs ADULTS"** or **"IT KNOWS 😱"**
* High contrast, orange and red on black, faces large. It has to read at phone size.

## 8. Voiceover script (full draft, about 7 min at a relaxed pace)

> *[COLD OPEN – no voice, just the scare]*
>
> But when a kid walks up… it knows the difference.
>
> I built a Halloween screen that sees who's coming up my driveway, figures out if they're a kid or an adult, and picks the right scare. It runs on an old Celeron PC, it needs no internet, and you can download it for free. Link's in the description.
>
> Here's what trick-or-treaters see, from the moment they turn into the driveway.
>
> When nobody's around, this pumpkin invites people in: "Trick or treat! Come on up!" The second someone steps into the driveway, it switches to a welcome. Kids get this cute little ghost. Adults get glowing eyes in the dark and a skull that whispers "come closer." And watch the eyes. They follow you.
>
> So how does it know if you're a kid? It uses a small AI model, running right on the PC, that finds people in the camera picture. Then it estimates your real height from where your feet are. Under 1.40 metres, you're a kid. And if kids and adults come together, it goes with the gentle version, so no little ones get traumatised.
>
> When you get to the candy, it reminds you: one per person. And the moment you reach into the jar… *[SCARE]*. For kids, it's a big friendly "BOO!" and a candy explosion. For adults… well, you saw.
>
> *[REACTIONS]*
>
> Want to build your own? You need four things, and you probably have them already: any Windows PC (mine's a cheap Celeron), a USB webcam, the biggest screen you've got, and speakers. No cloud, no subscription, nothing recorded.
>
> Go to the GitHub page (link in the description), click Releases, and download the Windows zip. Unzip it somewhere permanent, like C:\SpookyTreatStation. Windows will warn you because it's not signed by a big company. Click "More info", then "Run anyway". It's open source, so anyone can check the code.

If the camera sees your candy jar, switch on "Watch the candy jar" and put the blue box over it. Then have an adult stand far away, in the middle, and up close, and press Capture each time. >
> You can choose how scary it gets (mild, medium, or maximum), turn scares off for kids completely, change everything the characters say, or drop in your own scary videos.
>
> A few tips: put the camera at about chest height looking down the path, turn on a porch light so it can see at night, and set it to start with Windows so it's running when you get home.
